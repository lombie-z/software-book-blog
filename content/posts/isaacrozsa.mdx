## A playground for the music

I put my music on isaacrozsa.com, and I use the site as much to play with 3D and shaders as to host the songs. The singles share the home page, and each album gets a world of its own.

## The singles

The home page is a run of scenes, one per single: Prologue's cover glowing over a dark sea, then Dude Like Dust, then a red rozsa form, then links. The page doesn't scroll: flick the wheel, swipe or press an arrow key and it glides to the next scene, while the roses in the top right show where you are.

## Good Talk

In Good Talk you scroll the camera through a field of flowers and leaves. I gave each track its own colour, and the scene and the player in the corner change colour as you move from one song to the next. On each beat, a ripple goes out in that song's colour.

## Arrhythmia

Arrhythmia is early-2000s PC nostalgia. You switch it on with a power button, and the photos come through VHS noise and glitches, like they're on the old family computer. Each scroll adds a point to an MS Paint-style lasso around part of a photo, and when the loop closes, the cut-out dissolves into the next photo. If you keep scrolling past the last one, the machine blue-screens.

## Under the hood

- Scene changes. On the main sequence I catch wheel, touch and key events and turn each one into an eased move to the next scene, so the page itself stays put. Arrhythmia adds momentum, so a long flick keeps drawing before it settles.
- Performance. I render the liquid scenes on demand at 30fps, which cuts their GPU cost about in half, and pause them when they're off screen. Phones get a lighter raymarched shader with fewer steps and fewer blobs.
- The lasso editor. I drew each selection path by hand, on a hidden editor page that loads a photo and lets me click points around it. The page saves those points as the album's data.
