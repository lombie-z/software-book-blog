## A playground for the music

isaacrozsa.com is where my music lives, and it's as much a place to play with 3D and shaders as a place to listen. The singles share the home page, and each album gets a world of its own.

## The singles

The home page is a run of scenes, one per single: Prologue's cover glowing over a dark sea, then Dude Like Dust, then a red rozsa form, then links. It doesn't scroll. A flick of the wheel, a swipe or an arrow key glides you to the next scene, and the roses in the top right show where you are.

## Good Talk

Good Talk is a field of flowers and leaves that the camera flies through as you scroll. Each track has its own colour, so the whole scene shifts as you move from one song to the next, and the player in the corner shifts with it. Beats in the music send out ripples in that song's colour.

## Arrhythmia

Arrhythmia is early-2000s PC nostalgia. You switch it on with a power button, and the photos come through VHS noise and glitches, like they're on the old family computer. Each scroll draws an MS Paint-style lasso around part of a photo, point by point, until it cuts out and dissolves to the next. Keep going and the machine blue-screens.

## Under the hood

- Scenes, not scrolling. On the main sequence I don't want the page to scroll at all, so wheel, touch and key events are caught and each one moves to the next scene with an eased transition. Arrhythmia adds momentum, so a long flick keeps drawing before it settles.
- Performance. The liquid scenes render on demand at 30fps instead of every frame, which roughly halves their GPU cost, and they pause completely when they're off screen. Phones get a lighter version of the raymarched shader, with fewer steps and fewer blobs.
- The lasso editor. Every selection path is hand-drawn. A hidden editor page loads a photo and lets me click the lasso out over it, and those points become the album's data.
