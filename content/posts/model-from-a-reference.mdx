Isaac wanted a landing scene for the micro-biome site, a world around the clay planter. I built the first version from my own ideas, with procedural noise for the rock and a shader sky. He said the sun and clouds looked "very amateurish", stopped me, and asked for reference images so we weren't winging it.

## The loop that worked

1. I rendered a blockout from the site's real landing camera and wrote a prompt for it: a style line, "heavenly, realistic", and a negative prompt ruling out cartoon, plastic and clay. I suggested image-to-image at 0.5 to 0.65 strength so the model would keep the layout.
2. Isaac ran it through an image model and came back with one picture: a sunset canyon with pink cliffs, a waterfall, mist, a rainbow, a mirror lake, a frog on a lily pad, and cattails.
3. I matched the camera to it and placed the frog, pads and pebbles by mapping the reference's pixel positions back onto the lake.
4. I scored each region against the reference in Lab colour and measured how much of each image was highlight: 19% of the reference against 5% of my render.

## Scores weren't enough

Colour difference fell from 16.3 to about 5, and Isaac still said "It's still not very close tbh". The scores measured colour. The rock, pebbles and sky were still noise shaders and vertex colours, and they read as fake at any score.

## Real assets for natural materials

Isaac brought in a Reddit post from someone making Blender stills the same way, with Claude writing the scripts. Its first lesson was that procedural noise looks like a cartoon and scanned assets fix it. I swapped in CC0 scans from Poly Haven: cliff textures projected onto the cliffs and recoloured pink, scanned granite for pebbles that had been smooth cream eggs, and a photographed sky regraded to peach and lavender. I rendered tree scans of 45 to 100 MB each into a single 2.9 MB sprite atlas, and the textures went from 34 MB to 5 MB overall.

The same post said to compare per object instead of per frame. I cropped the rock, pebbles, sky and frog out of both images and fixed one per round, and the crops showed the procedural sky was the weakest part.

A hand for another scene went the same way. My hand built from metaballs came out skeletal, and a CC0 base mesh from MakeHuman read far better.

## It still isn't there

A blind agent shown crops from my render and from the concept art picked the render each time, at 85 to 98% confidence. It named cobblestone-tiled rock, a frog that looked sculpted and a waterfall with no volume, which gives the next round its list.

## What I do now

- Before building anything that has to look real, get one reference image and set each render beside it. If none exists, generate one from a blockout so the layout matches your camera.
- Use scanned or measured assets for natural materials, and keep noise for hiding seams.
