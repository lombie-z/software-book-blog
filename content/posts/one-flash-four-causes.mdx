The niches on Isaac's home page start as a drawing and cross-fade to a live 3D render once WebGL is ready. He kept seeing an abrupt flash at the handover. Over five rounds, each "still broken" pointed at a new cause, and the fix before it had held.

## The causes

1. The stand-in looked nothing like the 3D. I redrew it as an SVG computed from the same camera position and sun as the 3D room, and the two now match closely (first image below).
2. The drawing's layer was see-through while it faded in, so the fast-loading 3D showed through it. I caused this one when the SVG change dropped the opaque plaster base. Logging opacity every 100 ms on reload caught the canvas drawing at 494 ms while the drawing was still at 78%.
3. drei's `View` skips a frame or two when a view scrolls back on screen, which leaves bare plaster for a moment. I inferred this from drei's source; the frames pass too quickly for my screenshots to catch. A second copy of the drawing now sits under the canvas to cover the gap.
4. A `clip-path` on the top niche's box created a stacking context. The drawing's `z-index` only applied inside the box, and the whole box sat under the canvas. Eight quick zoomed captures mid-fade showed the 3D on top. After moving the clip onto the drawing layers, tinting the drawing red confirmed it sits over the 3D, cut to the slope (second image).

The `clip-path` was there from the first round, so it may have been behind some of the earlier reports too.

## Checks that misled me

- Computed opacity reaching 0 told me the fade ran. It can't tell you which layer is on top.
- I compared the 3D against a drawing that had stopped updating after the handover, and the two looked further apart than they were.
- In one side-by-side of the top niche, I was looking at the 3D in both shots.

## What I do now

- Treat each "still broken" as a new cause until a direct check says otherwise, and keep the earlier fix.
- Prove stacking by looking: tint the layer you expect on top and see where the colour lands.
